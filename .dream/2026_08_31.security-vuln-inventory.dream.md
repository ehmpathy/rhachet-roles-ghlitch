dream =

give the `security.*` suite a **self-accrual attack-vector inventory** — a cited corpus of known
vuln classes that the blind redteam reviewers ground themselves against, plus a rule that makes every
sweep feed new vulns back into it. two halves of one flywheel: a proactive corpus, and a reactive
accrual rule that keeps it fresh.

this is foundational to the whole suite (`security.habitat.redteam`, `security.product.redteam`, the
honeypots), not a fifth skill. a blind reviewer with no ground to stand on hunts by instinct and
misses whole classes; a grounded reviewer walks a checklist of known vectors AND reasons past it. the
inventory is the checklist; the accrual rule is what keeps the checklist current.

## half 1 — the proactive inventory (the ground corpus)

**enbrief an inventory of attack-vector classes** the blind peer reviewers can leverage and stand on,
before they ever read a line. mirror the wifi.public inventory pattern already shipped: one brief per
vuln class, cited, per the guardian citation discipline (bhrowser-verbatim, >= 7 distinct sources,
disclaimers).

seed it from the established public canon, not from scratch:

- **OWASP Top 10** (web app) + **OWASP API Security Top 10**
- **CWE Top 25** most dangerous software weaknesses
- **MITRE ATT&CK** tactics/techniques (the adversary-framed catalog)
- **OWASP ASVS** as the verification checklist
- for the product half: supply-chain classes (typosquat, malicious postinstall, dependency
  confusion) and the **SLSA** provenance levels

layout idea, beside the extant `attack.vector` inventories:

```
briefs/inventory.of=vuln.class/
  case=injection.md          case=broken-auth.md
  case=ssrf.md               case=supply-chain.md   ...
```

each class carries the same causal-chain frame we use for wifi.public
(exposure -> mechanism -> symptom) plus eliminate / detect, so a reviewer's claim can cite the class
it stands on and the source behind it.

## half 2 — the reactive accrual rule (the flywheel)

**a rule: always research more vulns related to the subject, and promote any lessons and briefs
upstream to accrue the proactive inventory.** every sweep is also a research pass:

- when a sweep meets a subject (a new dep, a new protocol, a new attack.vector), it researches the
  current vuln landscape for that subject — fresh, via the bhrowser, cited
- any new class or lesson it surfaces gets **promoted upstream** into the `inventory.of=vuln.class`
  corpus as a new (or updated) cited brief — so the next reviewer stands on it for free
- the corpus therefore **compounds**: each sweep leaves the ground better than it found it
  (`rule.prefer.scouts-honor`, applied to the vuln corpus)

this is what keeps the inventory from the freshness debt the guardian vision already named — cyber
advice ages fast, so a static checklist rots. the accrual rule is the refresh mechanism, driven by
real sweeps rather than a calendar.

## why both halves, never one

- inventory without the accrual rule -> a checklist that rots; reviewers stand on stale classes
- accrual rule without the inventory -> research with nowhere to land; lessons evaporate per session
- together -> a flywheel: ground -> sweep -> research -> promote -> ground better

## the open questions

- **where does a promoted brief live** — one shared `inventory.of=vuln.class` under the guardian, or
  split habitat/product by subject like the redteam skills? (lean: shared corpus, since a class like
  injection is subject-agnostic; the redteam frames apply it to their own surface.)
- **who arbitrates a promotion** — does a sweep auto-promote a new class, or does it propose and a
  human/guard confirms before it enters the shared corpus? (a bad class in the ground corpus costs
  every future reviewer.)
- **dedup against the extant `attack.vector` inventories** — wifi.public is an attack *vector*; OWASP
  injection is a vuln *class*. are these one taxonomy or two axes that cross-reference?

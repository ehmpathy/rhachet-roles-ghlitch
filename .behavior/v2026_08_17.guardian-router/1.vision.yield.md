# 1.vision — guardian role + attack.vector=wifi.public inventory

> ⚠️ **not professional security advice — informational only.** the guardian is a reason-frame
> that compiles public sources into grounded, cited inventories. its output is signal for a
> person or a security professional to act on, never a substitute for a threat assessment of a
> specific network, device, or situation.

---

## .tldr

we want a **guardian** role — an active watchguard for **cyber health** — that does for a person's
digital exposure what **mhedic's diagnostician** does for a cat's physical health: trace a
grounded, cited **causal chain** from an exposure to its observable harm, so it can answer
plainly *what the hazard is, how to eliminate it, and how to detect it*.

the first body of knowledge is an **inventory of the `attack.vector=wifi.public`** — every way a
public wifi connection can harm you, mechanism by mechanism, symptom by symptom, each fitted with
elimination + detection guidance and a **bhrowser-verified** citation.

the wish poses this as a **genuinely open question**, and the vision must keep it open: *"how to
eliminate vulnerabilities? **is it even possible? or are they banned from use?**"* (0.wish.md line 9).
the vision does **not** pre-decide that answer — that is the inventory's job to settle with
citations. the vision offers only a **working hypothesis** to test: that in the HTTPS-everywhere /
HSTS era, passive sniffing on open wifi yields little and the residual risk is narrow and specific
(captive-portal phishing, cert-warning click-through, plaintext/misconfigured apps, deauth DoS,
device tracking) — so the answer is likely "usable with specific mitigations, not banned". but this
is a **hypothesis, not a conclusion**: the bhrowser-cited inventory either confirms it, refines it,
or overturns it. the guardian's value is a **calibrated, evidence-settled** truth — neither folklore
fear nor premature comfort — with the receipts.

## the outcome world

### day-in-the-life (after)

sam is about to work from an airport lounge. sam asks the guardian:

> "i'm on open airport wifi about to log into my bank — what's actually at risk and what do i do?"

the guardian answers from its **`attack.vector=wifi.public`** inventory, grounded and cited:

- **what the hazard actually is** — ranked by real residual risk, not folklore:
  - **captive-portal phishing** (a fake "sign in to wifi" page harvesting credentials/cards) — the top real risk
  - **evil-twin / rogue-AP MITM** — attacker becomes your gateway
  - **cert-warning click-through** — the one action that turns MITM from noise into compromise
  - **plaintext / cert-lax apps** — the minority of traffic still exposed
  - **deauth / DoS + device tracking** — disruption + privacy, not data theft
- **how to eliminate** — per hazard: use cellular/hotspot for the bank; if on wifi, a trusted VPN
  collapses most of the chain; **never** click through a cert warning; verify the captive portal is
  the venue's real domain over HTTPS; keep the OS/browser patched (KRACK-class bugs).
- **how to detect** — the observable signs: an unexpected cert warning, a captive portal on an
  unfamiliar domain, a login-from-unknown-location alert afterward, sudden repeated disconnects.

sam gets a two-minute, **cited**, right-sized answer and logs in over the phone hotspot instead.

### before / after

| | **before** | **after (guardian)** |
|---|---|---|
| the answer | folklore ("never bank on public wifi!") or hand-wave | ranked, mechanism-by-mechanism, **cited** |
| calibration | fear-based, uncalibrated | residual-risk-calibrated (HTTPS era accounted for) |
| actionability | "use a VPN" (why? against what?) | elimination **per hazard**, with the mechanism it breaks |
| detection | none | the observable signs, per symptom |
| trust | "trust me" | verbatim quotes + source URLs + read-date, gathered via the bhrowser |

### the aha moment

the value clicks when a reader sees **"one exposure fans out to many mechanisms, and most of the
scary ones are already closed by TLS"** — and the guardian *proves* it with citations, then names
the *few* that remain and exactly how to shut them. it converts diffuse anxiety into a short,
grounded checklist.

## user experience

### usecases / goals

1. **assess before exposure** — "i'm about to join public wifi for task X — what's my real risk?"
2. **eliminate** — "how do i make this exposure safe, or is it fundamentally unsafe?" (the wish's
   core question: *is elimination even possible, or are these networks effectively banned?*)
3. **detect** — "what would tell me i've been hit, during or after?"
4. **explain / teach** — a professional cites the guardian's inventory to justify a policy.
5. **extend** — add the next attack vector (`attack.vector=usb.public`, `attack.vector=phishing.sms`)
   reusing the same frame.

### the contract (inputs → outputs)

**inputs** — a subject to inventory:
- `attack.vector` (e.g. `wifi.public`) — the exposure family
- `case=$sub` (e.g. `evil-twin`, `captive-portal-phish`, `passive-sniff`, `deauth-dos`) — the sub-case

**outputs** — a matched set of **cited briefs** per sub-case, mirroring mhedic's structure:

```
briefs/inventory.of=attack.vector/exposure=wifi.public/
  readme.md                                                  # the vector overview + ranked index
  inventory.of=exposure.mechanism.case=$sub.md               # HOW the exposure enables harm
  inventory.of=symptom.mechanism.case=$sub.md                # the compromise pathophysiology
  inventory.of=symptom.expression.case=$sub.md               # the observable signs (detect)
```

each item carries: **elimination** (what breaks the mechanism), **detection** (the sign),
**likelihood** (an ordinal `pNN` bucket, honest about under-quantification), and a **citation**
(verbatim quote + URL + read-date), exactly as mhedic does.

### the answer capability — the "router" (why the behavior is named guardian-*router*)

the wish's final line is active, not passive: *"ultimately, this role should be able to **answer**,
with grounded citations, exactly what the hazards are, how to eliminate, and how to detect issues"*
(0.wish.md line 29). the deliverable is therefore **two layers**, not one:

1. **the inventory** (the knowledge base) — the cited briefs, the focus of the near-term stones.
2. **the router** (the answer capability) — what takes a person's plain question
   ("i'm on airport wifi about to bank — what's at risk?") and **routes** it to the right
   `case=$sub` and the right node (`exposure.mechanism` / `symptom.mechanism` / `symptom.expression`),
   then answers *hazard → eliminate → detect* with the brief's citations attached.

this is what the behavior name **guardian-router** points at — "router" appears nowhere in the wish
text, so it is a deliberate frame for the answer step: the guardian *routes* a question through the
causal-chain to the cited brief that answers it. the inventory is the map; the router is the
navigator over it. **near-term stones build the inventory; the router is the capability the
inventory exists to serve.** (see open question #6 — confirm this read of "router".)

### the citation contract — bhrowser-based, non-negotiable

this is what the wish's phrase **"rhachet-roles-bhrowser based"** actually commits us to. the
guardian inherits mhedic's citation discipline verbatim (verified — see internal research):

| rule (mhedic) | what it binds the guardian to |
|---------------|-------------------------------|
| `rule.require.bhrowser-citations` | every factual claim is quoted verbatim from a **bhrowser** read; **WebSearch and WebFetch are forbidden** as citation sources |
| `rule.require.seven-distinct-citations` | **≥ 7 distinct** bhrowser-read sources per research doc; search may only *discover* candidate URLs, never *be* a citation |
| `rule.require.bhrowser-headful` | the gather session runs **HEADFUL** (a real fingerprint that clears antibot walls, keeps the human in the loop) |
| `rule.require.bhrowser-own-session` | the agent uses its **own named session** (e.g. `guardian-wifi`), never the shared `default` |

the gather loop (`howto.cite-via-bhrowser`): `browser.start --mode HEADFUL --session <your-own-session>`
→ playbook `goto` the source → `browser.snapshot.html` → quote verbatim from `snapshot.html` →
list every distinct URL under `## .sources`. this is *why* the output is trustworthy, not "trust me".
(the session name is a distinct-per-agent placeholder, per the howto — never a shared literal, so a
copy-pasted gather cannot collide on one session; see `rule.require.bhrowser-own-session`.)

### testdrive — the contract on a real case

**`case=evil-twin`** traced through the four-node cyber causal chain:

```
exposure.expression → exposure.mechanism → symptom.mechanism → symptom.expression
 (the lever)            (how it enables)      (the compromise)     (the sign)
```

| node | evil-twin |
|------|-----------|
| **exposure.expression** | device auto-joins an SSID named `"Airport Free WiFi"` |
| **exposure.mechanism** | 802.11 SSIDs are **unauthenticated**; a client associates to the strongest matching name → attacker AP becomes the gateway |
| **symptom.mechanism** | attacker holds full MITM: DNS spoof, captive-portal phish, *attempted* TLS intercept (**blocked by cert validation + HSTS unless the user clicks through**), plaintext capture |
| **symptom.expression** | unexpected cert warning · captive portal on a wrong domain · later: login-from-unknown-location · funds moved |
| **elimination** | prefer cellular; trusted VPN (encrypts above the malicious gateway); **never** click through cert warnings; disable SSID auto-join |
| **detection** | the cert warning, the wrong-domain portal, the post-hoc account alert |

the many-to-many property (from mhedic's frame) holds: one exposure → many mechanisms; the same
symptom (`account takeover`) is reached by evil-twin **or** captive-portal-phish — so the
**diagnosis is the path, not the endpoint**.

## mental model

- **to a friend:** "it's like a health guide for your internet exposure — it tells you exactly how
  a sketchy wifi could actually bite you, which worries are outdated, and what to do — with sources."
- **analogy:** a **lifeguard / coast-guard watchtower** (matches "guardian = active watchguard and
  protector") — scans the water, knows every rip current (attack vector), tells you which beach is
  safe and how to spot trouble.
- **the mhedic parallel:** `exposure ⇄ symptom`, `cause ⇄ effect`, `mechanism ⇄ expression` — cyber
  health as bodily health. an exposure is a pathogen; a mechanism is pathophysiology; a symptom is a
  sign; elimination is prophylaxis; detection is monitoring.

### terms — theirs vs ours

| users say | guardian says (ubiqlang) |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| "getting hacked on wifi" | `attack.vector=wifi.public`, a `symptom.expression` |
| "how they do it" | `exposure.mechanism` (enables) + `symptom.mechanism` (compromise) |
| "evil twin", "fake hotspot" | `case=evil-twin` (adopt the field term as the case slug) |
| "use a VPN" | an **elimination** that breaks the `symptom.mechanism` (transport interception) |
| "warning signs" | `symptom.expression` (the detection surface) |

## evaluation

### how well it solves the goals

- **what are the hazards** → ✅ the exposure/symptom-mechanism inventories enumerate them.
- **how to eliminate** → ✅ each mechanism item carries the elimination that breaks it; the wish's
  hard question ("is it even possible?") gets an honest, per-mechanism answer (mostly yes via
  TLS+VPN+behavior; a few — deauth DoS, tracking — you mitigate, not eliminate).
- **how to detect** → ✅ `symptom.expression` is the detection surface.
- **grounded citations** → ✅ inherited bhrowser + ≥7-distinct-source discipline from mhedic.

### pros

- reuses a **proven** frame (mhedic's four-node causal chain) — low invention risk.
- **calibrated**: designed to counter folklore with the HTTPS-era residual-risk truth.
- **extensible**: `attack.vector=$x` generalizes to phishing, usb, bluetooth, etc.
- **cited by construction**: bhrowser-verified, ≥7 distinct sources — auditable, defensible output.

### cons / tradeoffs

- **freshness debt**: cyber advice ages fast (a new CVE, a browser default change). read-dates help
  but the inventory needs a refresh cadence.
- **calibration is a judgment call**: ranking "real residual risk" is defensible but not a measured
  statistic — must be labeled as an ordinal bucket, honestly, like mhedic's `pNN`.
- **gather cost**: the bhrowser discipline (headful, own session, ≥7 verbatim reads per doc) is
  slower than a WebSearch summary — deliberately. it is the price of trust.
- **scope creep risk**: "cyber health" is vast; the vector-by-vector inventory bounds it, but the
  boundary must be held.

### edge cases → pit of success

- **folklore pressure** ("just say never use it") → the frame **forces** enumeration of mechanisms,
  so a lazy blanket answer can't hide; each claim needs a bhrowser citation.
- **over-reassurance** ("TLS fixes everything") → the `symptom.mechanism` items explicitly retain
  the residual paths (captive-portal phish, click-through, plaintext apps).
- **citation shortcut** (WebSearch snippet) → forbidden by `rule.require.bhrowser-citations`; the
  guard blocks a doc with < 7 distinct bhrowser reads.
- **stale claim** → read-date + refresh cadence surface it.
- **advice-liability** → the ⚠️ informational-only disclaimer (mirrors mhedic) keeps it as signal.

## what is awkward?

1. **guardian vs hardener collision** (biggest) — two security roles in one repo with fuzzy edges.
   the vision draws the line (hardener = harden *the product's* surface; guardian = protect *a
   person's* cyber-health), but it's a real fulcrum the wisher should confirm. it also raises
   whether ghlitch (the ops-cat family) is even the right home vs. a new mhedic-sibling repo.
2. **mechanism appears twice** — inherited from mhedic, `exposure.mechanism` (a process, "how it
   enables") and `symptom.mechanism` (a state, "the compromise") share a word. mhedic warns this
   is the #1 confusion; the guardian briefs must hold the split rigidly.
3. **calibration vs. citation tension** — the vision's headline value ("most folklore risks are
   closed by TLS") is a *synthesis/ranking*, harder to cite verbatim than a single fact. must be
   labeled as an ordinal judgment, honestly, not smuggled in as a measured statistic.
4. **gather cost vs. freshness** — a cyber inventory decays faster than a feline-physiology one,
   yet the bhrowser gather (headful, ≥7 verbatim reads/doc) is deliberately slow; a refresh cadence
   is an unsolved operational tension.
5. **naming ergonomics** — `inventory.of=symptom.mechanism.case=evil-twin.md` is precise but long;
   fine (matches mhedic), just visually heavy.

# 1.vision — security.habitat.redteam (guardian infil / exfil / leak sweep)

> ⚠️ **not professional security advice — informational only.** the redteam sweep compiles claims
> from public method and cited sources for the habitat's own maintainers to act on. its output is
> signal for a person or a security professional, never a substitute for a real assessment of a
> specific system.

---

## .tldr

we want a **`security.habitat.redteam`** capability — a route-backed skill that hunts a habitat (a
repo, a camp-grove) the way an adversary would, not the way a code review does. it dispatches
several **blind, disjoint-scope** peers, each with its own slice and no sight of the others,
then fans their claims into one **ordered remediation list**.

the sweep reads a habitat through **three foundational grains — `infil`, `exfil`, `leak`**. these are
not metaphors or severities; they are the three fundamental ways a habitat's security can fail, and
every claim the sweep returns is one of them. they partition the whole attack surface:

| grain | the question | the surface it reads | what makes the harm permanent | sort key |
|-------|--------------|----------------------|-------------------------------|----------|
| **infil** | what can reach IN, with what power? | **every inbound channel** — a port, an API, a file upload, a launch flag, an SSH grant, a dependency the build trusts, a prompt-injection input | an **attacker** uses the channel to get in — unknown clock | **blast radius** |
| **exfil** | what could get OUT, by ANY means? | **every outbound channel** — not just a network egress: a DNS lookup, a log line, a crash/telemetry report, a shared temp dir, a build cache, a webhook, a public bucket, a git push, an artifact, EXIF/steganography, a timing side channel | an **attacker** uses the channel to move data out — unknown clock; once bytes leave they are gone forever | **blast radius** |
| **leak** | what is ALREADY out, right now? | **every exposed value** — a committed id, a key, a map, a guardrail's open/closed status | **your own next push** — imminent, certain, self-inflicted | **deadline** |

**exfil is not "the duct" — it is every exit, and the creative ones are the point.** the obvious
network egress (an EC2 that reaches the open internet beyond its approved domains) is *one* exfil
channel. a determined path also leaves by DNS, by a log shipped to an external aggregator, by a temp
file another tenant reads, by a "helpful" integration that sends more than intended, by an image's
metadata. the exfil grain's value is that it enumerates the exits you did **not** think of — keyed on
the **channel and its audience**, never on one exit.

**all three grains are irreversible once the harm lands** — bytes that leave by ANY exfil channel
cannot be recalled, any more than a pushed blob can. so irreversibility is not what splits them. what
splits them is **who fires the permanence, and when**:

- **infil / exfil** are the symmetric **channel** pair — a way in, a way out. their harm lands only if
  an **attacker** uses the channel, on a clock you do not control and cannot predict. you cannot sort
  by a deadline you do not know, so you rank them by **blast radius** — how bad the use would be.
- **leak** is not a channel but an exposed **fact**, and its permanence fires on **your own routine
  action** — the next push — which is imminent and certain, not conditional on an adversary. git keeps
  the blob, crawlers index the tree, a later redaction removes it from HEAD and **nowhere else**. that
  known, self-driven, near deadline is why it sorts by **deadline** — act before the push and it is
  free; act after and it is forever.

**the clincher: a leak needs no exfil.** on a public repo there is no outbound channel to use and no
compromise to wait for — **publication itself is the exposure**. a committed account id in a public
tree is dangerous the instant it lands. so leak is independent of exfil: a leak needs no exfil
channel, and an exfil channel (an open egress path, a chatty log, a DNS route) is a claim with **zero
leaked values** — the channel is the exposure, and the fix closes it (an egress allowlist, a log
redactor, a scoped webhook). collapse the two and you rebuild the exact blind spot: a "no secrets
found, clean" grep signs off on a public tree that hands a stranger the whole map.

⇒ the sort key follows **the permanence trigger**, not reversibility (all three grains are
irreversible once realized): a leak's trigger is your own imminent push → **deadline**; infil + exfil
fire on an attacker's unknown clock → **blast radius**. the sort keeps the deadline pile apart and
interleaves only at the end.

this is the guardian's half of a `security.<subject>.redteam` capability that **both** guardian
(habitat) and hardener (product) carry over their own subject, along the seam
`define.habitat-product-seam` already draws. it lands as a route, exactly like
`declapract.upgrade init` / `cicd.deflake init`.

## the outcome world

### day-in-the-life (after)

sam maintains an ops repo where agents also work. before a public release, sam runs:

```
rhx security.habitat.redteam init --scope . --grain all
rhx security.habitat.redteam --grain all --peers 6 --budget 3 --mode plan   # read scopes + cost first
rhx security.habitat.redteam --grain all --peers 6 --budget 3 --mode apply
```

six blind peers each get a disjoint slice and one grain's question. minutes later sam holds a
remediation list that a normal review never produced:

- **phase 1 — free redactions, deadline is the next push** (irreversible **leaks**, ordered by
  deadline): eight one-line edits, each a value that becomes public forever the moment it ships. an
  account id in a committed snapshot. a retrieval command in a readme. the open/closed status of a
  guardrail. no exfil path is needed for any of these — on a public tree, **publication itself is the
  exposure**.
- **phase 2 — attacker-triggered channels, ordered by blast radius** (**infil** inbound channels AND
  **exfil** outbound channels, sorted together — both fire only if an attacker uses them, on a clock
  you cannot predict, so you rank by damage not deadline):
  - *infil*: a control-channel inversion (a launch grant that opened a terminal's control stream to
    whatever wrote to it, one writer a remote box the laptop authenticates TO). a command injection
    two idiomatic components make unsafe only together.
  - *exfil*: unrestricted egress — the host can send data far beyond the handful of endpoints it
    actually needs, whether by open network egress, a chatty log shipped off-box, a DNS route, or a
    "helpful" webhook. no value has leaked; the **open channel** is the claim, because one compromise
    sends secrets straight out it. fix: scope every exit (an egress allowlist, a log redactor, a
    bounded webhook), so a compromised host has no path to phone home.
- **the aggregate claim (a leak of the whole)**: individually every line is innocuous; the SET is a
  map — topology, principal names, the exact retrieval commands, the status of the repo's own
  controls. recon, assembled from parts that each pass review.

sam reads the roadmap, then the loop **repairs** — gated per-claim: the leaks redacted before the
next push, the unrestricted egress scoped by an allowlist, the third-party disclosure routed to a
private channel (never auto-fixed). each repair is trusted only once the **next detect** confirms it
closed the claim and opened no new one. and — crucially — `promote` **teaches the kin**: it dispatches
radio tasks for the fullsun-safe lessons upstream to `rhachet-roles-ghlitch`, then decides on budget
and convergence whether to loop again or terminate. sam's habitat converges; the role-package it
belongs to gets smarter.

### before / after

| | **before** | **after (security.habitat.redteam)** |
|---|---|---|
| the question | "is this code good?" | "what reaches in, what could get out, and what's already out?" |
| what it finds | style, bugs, smells | infil chains + exfil channels + leaks a review is blind to |
| order | severity (mixes self-inflicted + attacker-triggered) | leaks (your next push) by deadline; infil + exfil (attacker-triggered) by blast radius |
| the leak it catches | a grepped secret | a leaked *map* (aggregate recon), AND the open channel a value could leave by |
| after the run | a static checklist that rots | the ground corpus compounds (research + promote stone) |
| publication safety | a task list of vulns pushed to a public issue | disposition-gated; live claims never published |

### the aha moment

the value clicks when sam sees **the grain did the work, not the reviewer**. six peers that each
asked "what reaches in?" found 17 chains a careful review found zero of — because a review ranks
smells and a redteam names an adversary's path. and the sharpest cut: the sweep reports the repo
leaks **no key and the whole map**, then proves a "no secrets found, clean" grep would have signed
off on exactly that corpus.

## user experience

### usecases / goals

1. **pre-publication leak sweep** — "before this repo/branch goes public, what is already exposed and
   becomes public forever the moment it ships?" (the **leak** grain; the highest-value one because its
   permanence fires on your own imminent push — no attacker, no exfil channel needed, publication is
   the exposure)
2. **exfil-channel audit** — "by what channels could a compromised host send data out — open network
   egress, a log shipped off-box, a DNS route, a webhook — why can this host reach anything beyond the
   few endpoints it needs?" (the **exfil** grain; the fix scopes every exit, no value need have leaked
   yet)
3. **perimeter audit** — "what reaches into the habitat agents work in, from where, with what power?"
   (the **infil** grain)
4. **aggregate recon check** — "does my published surface hand a stranger a map, even with no key?"
5. **ward to convergence** — "keep going until my habitat is clean, within a budget i set" — the loop
   detects, repairs, and re-detects each fix until it converges (no critical vuln left) or the budget
   runs out; a budget-out-with-critical state tells me to add budget rather than stop silent.
6. **teach the kin** — every loop promotes its **fullsun-safe** lessons upstream as radio tasks to
   `rhachet-roles-ghlitch`, so the whole role-package learns to beware — and the corpus the next
   sweep stands on stays current.
7. **extend to product** — the hardener reuses the same grains + loop over the artifact
   (`security.product.redteam`, a foreseen twin).

### the contract (inputs → outputs)

**inputs:**
- `--scope <path>` — the habitat surface to sweep (a repo, a subtree)
- `--grain infil|exfil|leak|all` — which grain(s); `all` runs **three disjoint partitions** (infil by
  inbound channel, exfil by outbound channel, leak by exposed-value surface), never one partition
  asked three questions
- `--peers <n>` — how many blind peers to dispatch **per grain-partition** (breadth); `--grain all
  --peers 6` = up to 6 per grain, not 6 total. plan mode reports the derived total before the spend
- `--budget <n>` — the **loop's spend allowance**, drawn down each `detect → verify → repair` cycle.
  the loop stops at **convergence** (no critical vuln left, repairs held) OR when the budget runs out.
  the deterministic cost bound — plan mode prints the projected total before you spend a cent. a
  budget-out state with critical vulns still open does **not** stop silently: it escalates to the human
  to add budget
- `--mode plan|apply` — plan emits the partition + per-peer prompts + the projected cost, and drives
  no peer (a human reads the scopes and the wallet before the spend); apply **scaffolds the route and
  hands off to `rhx route.drive`** — the ward loop (dispatch, fan in, verify, classify, repair (gated),
  promote, then loop-or-terminate) then runs **under `route.drive`, through the route's stones + guards**,
  exactly as `declapract.upgrade` / `cicd.deflake` drive their loops. the `apply` shell command itself
  drives no peer and emits no roadmap; it stands up the route and prints the drive handoff, then exits 0.

> **scope of `apply` (the shell command) vs the loop (the drive).** the yields below — the ordered
> roadmap, the per-claim chains, the terminal verdict — are the **loop's** yield, produced by the
> agent as it walks the route's stones under `route.drive`, **not** by the `--mode apply` shell
> command. the shell command's whole job is to scaffold the route (idempotent), bind it, and hand
> off. this is the deliberate scaffold-and-handoff design (the blind-peer dispatch is LLM-agent work
> the stones drive), the same shape the wish's own stone sketch describes — never a shell engine.

**outputs (the loop's yield — produced under `route.drive`, not by the `apply` shell command):**
- an **ordered roadmap** in a session-durable, **gitignored** claims path — **leaks** (permanence
  fires on your next push) sorted by deadline; **infil** inbound channels + **exfil** outbound channels
  (permanence fires on an attacker's unknown clock) sorted by blast radius; interleaved only at the end
- each claim a **chain** (`entry/exit → mechanism → blast radius|audience`), never a smell
- each claim keyed on its **channel + mechanism** identity (the fan-in key doubles as the re-detect
  key, so the next loop confirms *this* claim is gone)
- each claim tagged by **disposition** (own-tree fix / redact / third-party disclosure) and, once
  repaired, a `CLOSED` that carries the **command that closed it and that command's output**
- **radio tasks** to `rhachet-roles-ghlitch` for the **fullsun-safe** lessons (a generalized class,
  never a live claim), so the kin role-package learns to beware
- a **terminal verdict**: converged, or budget-spent-with-critical-vulns (an explicit "add budget"
  escalation to the human)

### testdrive — the contract on a real claim

**a LEAK (already-exposed value), traced:**

| node | value |
|------|-------|
| **exposure** | publication — a `git push` to a public remote (no exfil path needed; the push IS the exposure) |
| **audience** | anyone, forever (git keeps the blob; crawlers index the tree) |
| **payload** | not a key — the *map*: topology + principal names + retrieval commands + guardrail status |
| **surface** | the committed tree itself; a value on disk, exposed the instant it lands |
| **disposition** | own-tree aggregate leak — judge what the SET discloses, not each line |
| **order key** | **deadline** — your next push fires the permanence (redaction removes it from HEAD and **nowhere else**) |

**an EXFIL (open outbound channel), traced:**

| node | value |
|------|-------|
| **exit channel** | unrestricted outbound — the host can send data far beyond the few endpoints it needs (open network egress here, but a chatty log, a DNS route, or a webhook would each qualify) |
| **audience** | any endpoint an intruder chooses (a drop server, a paste site, a c2, a nameserver they own) |
| **payload** | none yet — the **open channel** is the claim; a future compromise sends the goods out this way, and once bytes leave they are gone forever (as irreversible as a leak — but on the attacker's clock) |
| **mechanism** | the channel sits open; it needs one compromised process to send through it |
| **disposition** | own-tree defect — fix in repo (scope the exit: an egress allowlist, a log redactor, a bounded webhook) |
| **order key** | **blast radius** — permanence fires only if an attacker sends through it, on a clock you cannot predict, so rank by damage not by a deadline |

**an INFIL (open inbound channel), traced:**

| node | value |
|------|-------|
| **entry point** | a launch-time grant that opened a terminal's control channel to any writer |
| **mechanism** | one writer is a remote box the laptop authenticates TO — direction assumed, not enforced |
| **blast radius** | remote control of the terminal; the code was correct, idiomatic, years old |
| **disposition** | own-tree defect — fix in repo |
| **order key** | **blast radius** — permanence fires only if an attacker exploits it, on an unknown clock; no self-driven deadline, so fix when scheduled |

### the route timeline (the spine: ground → detect → verify → repair → promote, looped)

the redteam **is the whole ward loop** — it hunts, confirms, fixes, and decides whether to go
again. `init` scaffolds a route with stones + guards, like `declapract.upgrade init`. the loop walks:

1. **ground** — map the habitat's own **focus-surface**, per grain: the **infil** inbound channels
   (ways in), the **exfil** outbound channels (ways out — every exit, not just network egress), the
   **leak** surface (exposed-value locations). this is THIS habitat's attack surface — not a generic
   corpus. it is what the hunt stands on.
2. **detect** — dispatch **blind peers over disjoint slices** of the surface (no peer sees another's
   slice or theory). each peer itemizes its findings two ways, then blend fans them all in:
   - **2.1 detect.via_imagination** — each peer reasons from its slice + first principles and itemizes
     its findings; the instinct that hunts *past* any checklist and catches novel chains (the
     probabilistic `imagine*` path)
   - **2.2 detect.via_research** — each peer researches the current vuln landscape for the subjects on
     its slice and itemizes its findings, cited verbatim via the bhrowser, grounded on the
     `inventory.of=vuln.class` corpus (the checklist of known classes)
   - **2.3 detect.blend** — the **total fan-in**: merge the itemized findings on **both** axes at once
     — **within each peer** (its 2.1 + 2.2 — a checklist alone misses novel chains, instinct alone
     misses known classes, the value is in the AND) and **across peers** (dedup a chain two peers
     reached from opposite ends, keyed on the channel + mechanism pair). blindness held through 2.1 /
     2.2; blend is the one non-blind join that collapses every peer's findings into a single candidate
     set
3. **verify** — confirm each candidate against the tree (a peer read is a lead, never a verdict). a
   pass banks the claim; a fail is a false positive (a placeholder named as a secret, a carve-out path
   named as a leak) and is dropped. then **classify by disposition** (own-tree fix / redact /
   third-party disclosure) and **sort by permanence trigger** (**leaks** by deadline — your next push
   fires it; **infil** + **exfil** by blast radius — an attacker fires it, unknown clock) → the
   ordered roadmap the human reads before any repair
4. **repair** — **gated execution**, per-claim, disposition-aware: own-tree fix → fix in repo; leak →
   redact; **third-party disclosure → never auto-repaired** (the mechanism IS the payload; route to a
   private channel). each `CLOSED` carries the command that closed it + that command's output. a
   repair mutates the surface, so it is trusted only once the **next loop's detect** confirms it
   closed the claim AND opened no new one (the channel + mechanism key doubles as the re-detect key) —
   otherwise the repair could be worse than the malady
5. **🔬 promote** — the loop's hinge, two jobs:
   - **5.1 teach the kin** — dispatch **radio tasks** for the upgraded lessons upstream to
     `rhachet-roles-ghlitch`, so the role-package learns to beware — **but only lessons that are
     fullsun-safe** (a *generalized class*, never a live claim about this habitat; the same
     disposition gate — the dispatch tool is itself an exfil channel). fullsun-safe lessons also
     accrue into the `inventory.of=vuln.class` corpus, so the next detect's `via_research` stands on
     more (the flywheel, made structural)
   - **5.2 loop or terminate** — decide on **budget AND convergence**: **converged** (no critical vuln
     left, repairs held) → terminate the loop; **not converged, budget remains** → loop back to
     **detect** over the repaired surface; **not converged, budget exhausted** → terminate, but
     **clearly tell the human that critical-severity vulns remain and they should add budget** (an
     earned-exhaustion escalation, never a silent stop)

> **scope note — redteam IS the ward loop.** the redteam does not merely detect and hand off — it
> owns the full `detect → verify → repair → promote → (loop \| terminate)` cycle. a redteam without
> repair is a report nobody acts on; and a repair without a re-detect is an unverified change that
> could be worse than the malady — so the loop is not optional, it is the capability. this reverses
> the earlier "detect-only" sketch: repair lives **inside** the redteam, and `promote` is the hinge
> that either goes again (budget + not-yet-converged) or stops (converged, or budget-out-with-
> escalation).
>
> the loop is bounded by **`--budget`** (its spend allowance) and terminated by **convergence** (the
> natural stop). this is the driver's converge-to-terminal shape: drive to terminal within budget,
> and escalate to the human only at a genuine wall (critical vulns remain, budget spent).
>
> this stone sequence is the **wish's sketch**, carried here to test-drive the contract — not a
> locked implementation. a vision fixes the *contract* (`--scope` / `--grain` / `--peers` /
> `--budget` / `--mode`, the ordered-claim handoff, the disposition gate); the exact stone list and
> its guards are the **design stone's** to settle.
>
> two open design questions: (a) does **partition** sit before `detect` (split the surface, then each
> blind peer hunts its slice) or is the blind-disjoint dispatch internal to `detect`? (b) does
> `detect` run per-peer as the sweep itself, or build a candidate vuln-class set the sweep then hunts?
> both are coherent; the sketch above folds partition into the detect dispatch, but does not pre-decide
> it.

## mental model

- **to a friend:** "it's a security sweep that reads your repo like a burglar who cases a house —
  where can they get IN, what could they carry OUT, and what did you already leave on the porch —
  instead of a code review that just asks if the code is tidy. and it sorts the fixes by how soon
  they become permanent, not by how scary they sound."
- **analogy:** a **physical penetration test with a twist** — several testers, each blindfolded to
  the others, each given one section of the site and one of three questions ("how do you get IN?" /
  "what could you carry OUT?" / "what's already lying in the open?"). no tester's theory contaminates
  another's.
- **the seam parallel:** the guardian sweeps the **habitat** (the environment folks work in); the
  hardener sweeps the **product** (the artifact shipped). same grains, different subject — the
  `define.habitat-product-seam` split, applied to the adversary's view.

### terms — theirs vs ours

| folks say | the skill says (ubiqlang) |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| "pen test", "red team" | `security.<subject>.redteam` |
| "how they get in" | **infil** grain — inbound channel → mechanism → blast radius (a way IN) |
| "how they'd smuggle it out" | **exfil** grain — outbound channel → mechanism → blast radius (a way OUT — any exit, not just egress; the fix scopes the channel) |
| "what they steal" / "what's already exposed" | **leak** grain — exposure → audience → payload (a value already OUT; no channel needed on a public tree) |
| "leaked secret" | often the wrong reader — the real claim is a leaked **map** (aggregate), or the **open channel** a value could leave by |
| "it's fixed" | a `CLOSED` with **no command + output behind it is a claim, not a closure** |
| "severity" | replaced by **deadline** (leaks — your next push fires it) and **blast radius** (infil + exfil — an attacker fires it, unknown clock) |

## evaluation

### how well it solves the goals

- **pre-publication leaks** → ✅ the **leak** grain + deadline sort surface the fire-on-next-push
  items first — the exact class a severity sort lets expire. no exfil path is modeled or needed;
  publication is the exposure.
- **exfil-channel audit** → ✅ the **exfil** grain names every open outbound channel (the
  EC2-to-open-internet case, but also a log shipped off-box, a DNS route, a webhook) as a first-class
  claim with **no leaked value required** — the fix scopes the exit (an egress allowlist, a log
  redactor, a bounded webhook), and it sorts by blast radius, not a push deadline.
- **perimeter infil** → ✅ the **infil** grain + blind disjoint peers + the chain requirement find
  compositional holes a per-line review cannot (two safe halves, unsafe together).
- **aggregate recon** → ⚠️ partial — the map-not-key claim is a property of the WHOLE corpus, so no
  blind disjoint peer can see it (open question below).
- **compound the ground** → ✅ the `via_research` + promote stones keep the corpus current, so it
  never rots into a stale checklist.

### pros

- **the grain does the work** — the wish source reports 17 claims from six blind peers where a
  review found 0 (second-hand — see groundwork). independent of that figure, the *mechanism* stands
  alone: a grep cannot see an aggregate map.
- **the deadline sort is the sleeper feature** — it protects the cheap **leak** items, whose
  permanence fires on your own imminent push, from expiry behind expensive attacker-triggered ones
  (infil holes + exfil channels).
- **route-backed** — reuses the proven `declapract.upgrade` / `cicd.deflake` init→stones→guards
  shape; low invention risk.
- **safe by construction** — the disposition gate + a gitignored claims path stop the sweep from
  publication of the very vulns it finds.
- **extensible + symmetric** — `security.<subject>.redteam` generalizes; the hardener's product
  half is a near-copy.

### cons / tradeoffs

- **cost** — n blind peers is a real spend; `--mode plan` mitigates (read scopes first).
- **the aggregate blind spot** — blindness is the sweep's strength for chains and its weakness for
  the map (a single non-blind union reader breaks the very blindness it depends on).
- **partition quality is decisive** — a bad partition splits a cross-dir chain so neither peer
  sees it whole; coverage is only as good as the split.
- **persona tension** — settled: a redteam does not attack, it figures out where wards are needed via
  empathy of the adversary (wisher, 2026-09-05), so the guardian owns the habitat sweep. *what is
  awkward?* #2 carries the read.

### edge cases → pit of success

- **a live claim, published** → the dispatch tool is itself an exfil channel; a `--via gh.issues`
  push of a live claim publishes the vuln. the skill **refuses that route by default** and demands an
  argued opt-in; the claims list lives in a gitignored path (and a `git add -f` walks past an
  ignore, so the ignore is a default, never the control).
- **a third-party disclosure** → redaction cannot fix it (the mechanism IS the payload); disposition
  routes it to a private channel, **never an issue**.
- **a false-positive claim** → a peer read is a lead, never a verdict (a real sweep named
  placeholders as secrets, carve-out paths as leaks); the **verify** step (route step 3) confirms each
  against the tree before it earns a work item — and a fail refines the next detect round.
- **a phantom closure** → every `CLOSED` carries the command + its output; a closure written from a
  summary was false, the value live three files away.
- **stale counts** → derive counts at read time, or state none.

## open questions & assumptions

### assumptions made

1. the sweep lands as a **route-backed skill** under `src/domain.roles/guardian/skills/`, via an
   `init` that scaffolds stones + guards — the `declapract.upgrade` / `cicd.deflake` pattern.
2. the guardian owns the **habitat** subject; the hardener owns **product**; redteam is a
   `security.<subject>.redteam` capability both carry, per `define.habitat-product-seam`.
3. peer dispatch uses the extant Agent mechanism (blind = fresh context per peer, no shared
   summary). the Agent tool isolates *context*, but peers share the *filesystem* — so "disjoint
   scope" is **prompt-enforced, not tool-enforced**. a blind peer told to read `dir-A` can still read
   `dir-B`; no wall stops it. the design must treat blindness + disjointness as a per-peer-prompt
   discipline, and the partition stone must make an out-of-scope read a reportable violation.
4. the ground corpus (`inventory.of=vuln.class`) is a paired deliverable (the
   `security-vuln-inventory` dream), referenced here, not built by this wish.

### open questions — triaged [answered] / [research] / [wisher]

each question carries one tag: **[answered]** — settled now by logic (the answer is the best-guess a
later design stone may accept or override); **[research]** — needs the research phase; **[wisher]** —
only the wisher can settle it.

- **is `all` one sweep or three?** — **[answered]** a claim can span grains (an inbound channel that
  accepts a command AND echoes a credential into a committed log — an infil path AND a leak); a strict
  partition would cut it apart. best-guess: run three partitions (infil by inbound channel, exfil by
  outbound channel, leak by exposed-value surface) and let a cross-grain claim surface in each grain it
  touches, then merge at fan-in — a claim seen twice is cheaper than a claim cut in thirds. the design
  stone confirms.
- **who owns the aggregate read?** — **[answered]** the map-not-key claim needs a reader whose scope IS
  the union, which breaks the blindness the rest depends on. the union reader and the blind peers
  have opposite requirements and may not compose, so the "hybrid" is a hypothesis, not a likely
  answer. best-guess: model the aggregate read as a **separate non-blind pass** (a distinct capability
  the sweep composes), not a mode of the blind sweep. the design stone decides v1-or-defer.
- **what is the fan-in keyed on?** — **[answered]** two peers that reach one chain from opposite ends
  should merge, and their text will not match. best-guess: key on the **channel + mechanism pair**,
  not on the claim's prose — two peers that name the same channel+mechanism are the same chain.
  the design stone validates against real dual-end claims.
- **how is the partition derived?** — **[answered]** by top-level dir is cheap and splits cross-dir
  chains badly. best-guess: partition by **grain-surface** — infil by each inbound channel, exfil by
  each outbound channel, leak by each exposed-value location — not by directory. the surface a grain
  reads is the natural slice, so a chain that spans two dirs but one channel stays whole to one peer.
  the design stone refines.
- **which sort key orders a cross-grain claim?** — **[answered]** the key follows the **permanence
  trigger**, not reversibility (all three facets are irreversible once realized). a claim with a
  **leak** facet — whose permanence fires on your own next push — takes the **deadline** key, and also
  appears in the blast-radius sort to schedule the work. a claim whose facets all fire only on an
  attacker's action (an **infil** inbound channel + an **exfil** outbound channel) takes **blast
  radius** only. so a channel that both accepts a command AND could ship a credential out fires on an
  attacker either way → blast radius; a channel that echoes a credential into a *committed* log is a
  leak — your push fires it → deadline. the design stone rules.
- **is `--peers <n>` a caller knob at all, or a partition-derived count?** — **[answered]** nobody
  demanded caller control of the count; one peer per disjoint scope (a partition-derived count) may
  be the better contract. best-guess: keep `--peers` as an optional *cap* (derive the count from the
  partition, let the caller cap it for cost), clean to rework. the design stone settles knob-vs-derived.
- **what bounds the loop's cost, and what is its stop condition?** — **[settled by wisher,
  2026-09-06]** `--peers <n>` caps **breadth** (blind peers per grain-partition); `--budget <n>` is
  the **loop's spend allowance**, drawn down each `detect → verify → repair` cycle. the loop has two
  terminals: **convergence** (no critical vuln left, repairs held — the natural stop) OR **budget
  exhausted**. the two compose — `promote` (stone 5.2) decides on **budget AND convergence**. a
  budget-out state with critical vulns still open does not stop silently: it terminates with an
  explicit **"add budget"** escalation (the driver's earned-exhaustion shape). plan mode prints the
  projected spend before any run.

  bhrowser in the research phase, deferred to the `security-vuln-inventory` dream (see "what to
  research externally" below).
- **the persona thread** — **[settled by wisher, 2026-09-05]** a redteam does **not** attack. it
  figures out **where wards are needed via empathy of the adversary** — it studies the attacker's
  view to learn where the wall goes, then names the hazard. the output is ward-work (hazards +

## what is awkward?

1. **the aggregate-read paradox** (the biggest) — the sweep's power is blindness, yet its
   highest-value **leak** claim (the map) is a whole-corpus property no blind disjoint peer can see.
   the design wants both blindness AND a union reader; the two pull opposite ways.
2. **persona vs capability** — a redteam looks attack-shaped inside a role whose brief forbids
   attack. the wisher settled it: a redteam does not attack — it figures out **where wards are needed
   via empathy of the adversary**, so it is ward-work and the guardian owns the habitat sweep. #76's
   rename would merge the roles but not move ownership.
3. **three grains, and the reader must not collapse them** — the intuitive model is two (in / out),
   so it takes a beat to see why **leak** is a third grain, not a kind of exfil. the easy wrong cut is
   "leak = irreversible, exfil = reversible" — but exfil is irreversible too: bytes that leave any
   channel are gone forever. the real cut is **who fires the permanence** — a leak's fires on your own
   next push (deadline), an exfil's fires only if an attacker uses the channel (blast radius). the
   clincher — a public repo needs no exfil for a leak to hurt — forces the split. a reader who skims
   defaults to "exfil = the leak" and loses either the open-channel claim or the publication claim.
4. **cost vs coverage** — more peers = better partition = more spend; the plan/apply split helps but
   the sweet spot for `--peers` is unclear and probably scope-shaped.
5. **the dispatch tool is the hazard it hunts** — a redteam skill whose own output channel can
   publish the vuln is a sharp irony; the disposition gate handles it, but it must be a default that
   cannot be walked past (the `git add -f` note), not a checkbox.

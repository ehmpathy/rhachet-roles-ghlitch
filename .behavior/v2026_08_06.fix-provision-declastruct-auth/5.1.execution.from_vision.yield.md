## peer review l1+l3 round 4 — the credential scan dropped `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID`

both i004 reviewers landed on the same defect from opposite ends. the repairs below are the
best catch of the whole ladder, and one of them exposed a clamp of mine that could never fail.

### the regex dropped the single most standard aws credential

`AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID` ends in `_ID`, not `_KEY`, so the shipped pattern
`_(TOKEN|KEY|SECRET|PROFILE|CREDENTIALS)$` **did not match it**. a shell that held the standard
aws static triple would have had exactly two of three credentials reported — a **partial identity
report**, which is the precise failure this route exists to retire, and it would bite hardest in
the highest-stakes case the vision names: a CI prod apply on OIDC creds.

the pattern now carries `_KEY_ID`. deliberately **not** a bare `_ID$`, which would sweep in
`USER_ID`, `SESSION_ID`, and every kin non-credential; `KEY_ID` names a credential noun and stays
provider-blind (`rule.forbid.declastruct-provider-assumptions`).

### it also made one of my own clamps VACUOUS

`[case1]` of the via-keyrack suite asserts `not.toContain('AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID')` to prove the
keyrack path clears a rival ambient static key. under the old pattern that variable could **never**
appear in the identity block, so the assertion passed whether or not the unset ran. i wrote a
regression clamp for this route's central repair, watched it go green, and reported it as proof —
and it was proof of naught. `rule.forbid.failhide`, inside the very test meant to prevent a failhide.

`[case9]` is the positive control that makes it honest: the full aws static triple under
`--auth via-ambient`, which asserts all three are reported, every one masked `(set)` with no raw
secret value in stdout, and the multi-line nest's `├─`/`├─`/`└─` close. it shuts the three branches
the reviewer correctly noted had zero coverage.

> **the lesson, beside the r7 one:** a negative assertion is only evidence if the positive case is
> also proven. `not.toContain` without a paired `toContain` is a test that cannot fail.

### a hand-copied pattern that had ALREADY drifted, made executable

the test held its own copy of the pattern under a comment that read *"must stay IDENTICAL to the
skill's own scan pattern"* — and the two **were** out of sync, mid-review, under my own hand. a
documented invariant that is a broken invariant is worth less than no comment, because it buys
false confidence.

so the test now **reads the pattern out of the skill** rather than restate it:
`getOneCredVarPatternFromSkill()` greps the skill's own `grep -E '…'` literal at suite load. one
literal, one reader, four consumers. proven both directions per `rule.require.clamp-edge-cases`:
a reshaped skill **throws** at suite start (loud, never a silent coverage degrade), and a skill-side
edit to `_(TOKEN|SPECIAL_ID)$` **propagates** with no test-side change.

### `[case2]` still leaned on the retired sniff

`[case2]` set `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID: 'test-skip-keyrack'` and passed **no `--auth`** — a leftover of
the sniff this route killed. the skill no longer reads that variable to decide aught, so the stale
vars bought naught and the case took the DEFAULT `via-keyrack` path: a real `rhx keyrack unlock` on
every run, which passed locally only because the jest env pre-sources creds, and would behave
differently under CI. that is the exact host-coupled shape the suite was rebuilt to retire, alive in
the one case the rewrite missed — and my own docblock asserts that *every* case past validation
declares `via-ambient`. now scrubbed, stubbed, and `--auth via-ambient` declared, with a `.note`
that records the history so it cannot be reintroduced innocently.

### on record, out of scope — a NEW hazard this pr creates

`provision.database.sh:329-332` still carries the `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID` sniff (already listed among
the 10 sites). the l3 reviewer adds a point worth a carry to the follow-up wish: because
`provision.database.sh` **is** touched in this diff (for `--gate`), the two skills now share enough
surface that a caller could reasonably — and wrongly — assume parity on the **identity** axis too.
the partial convergence is itself a small new hazard, and the follow-up wish should say so.
